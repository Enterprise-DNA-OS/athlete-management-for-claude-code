# Compliance: the rules this system checks

`/compliance` (or `npm run athletes -- compliance`) checks the records against the rules below and reports every breach with the rule and its source. They are written for a New Zealand or Australian programme: a pro or provincial side, a national or state squad, an academy, a university or school high-performance team. Set the numbers to yours when you install it (`npm run athletes -- settings --community_min_days=21 --symptom_free_days=14 --elite_symptom_free_days=10 --concussion_review_count=3`). Your sport's own concussion policy can be stricter than the national guidance: if it is, use its numbers.

This is a records check. It finds what the records say. It is not medical or legal advice, and it does not replace your doctor, your sport's medical policy, the Privacy Commissioner or the agency that runs your safety checks. Confirm each rule against the current version of its source before you rely on it, and when a rule changes, change the setting or the check (`complianceRows` in `scripts/athletes.mjs`) and this page in the same edit.

| Rule | What a breach looks like in the data | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Health information consent | An active athlete with no `consent_on` date | New Zealand: the Health Information Privacy Code 2020, rule 3, requires the athlete be told what health information is collected, why, and who will see it ([privacy.org.nz](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-act-2020/codes-of-practice/hipc2020/)). Australia: Australian Privacy Principle 3.3 allows sensitive information, which includes health information, to be collected only with consent and where it is reasonably necessary ([oaic.gov.au](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-3-app-3-collection-of-solicited-personal-information)). A signed consent on file covers both |
| R2 No match before the return date and a clearance | A match session with a load for a concussed athlete after the injury day and before the competition date, or before a recorded clearance | ACC's National Concussion Guidelines: a minimum of 21 days away from full competition, counting the day of injury as day 0, and a medical clearance before return to play ([acc.co.nz](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/Uploads/National-concussion-guidelines-v4.pdf)). The AIS Concussion and Brain Health Position Statement 2024 sets the same 21 days for under-19s and community sport, and at least 14 symptom-free days before contact training ([ausport.gov.au](https://www.ausport.gov.au/concussion)) |
| R3 No contact training before the symptom-free days | A contact session (not a match) with a load for a concussed athlete before `symptoms_resolved_on` plus the days needed | AIS 2024 position statement: 14 days symptom free at rest before contact training for under-19s and anyone without a dedicated doctor or physio; for adult high-performance athletes with daily medical cover, at least 10 symptom-free days ([ausport.gov.au](https://www.ausport.gov.au/concussion)). The check uses the 10-day path only for athletes 19 or over in a squad marked `daily_medical` |
| R4 A doctor clears every concussion | A concussion marked cleared with no `cleared_by`, or cleared by a staff member whose role is not doctor | ACC National Concussion Guidelines: medical clearance before return to play ([acc.co.nz](https://www.acc.co.nz/assets/Uploads/National-concussion-guidelines-v4.pdf)); AIS 2024: cleared by a healthcare practitioner before full contact training ([ausport.gov.au](https://www.ausport.gov.au/concussion)) |
| R5 Stages in order, at least a day apart | A return-to-play stage recorded with the stage before it missing, or on the same day as the stage before it | The Amsterdam 2022 consensus statement on concussion in sport sets out the return-to-sport strategy step by step, each step typically taking at least 24 hours ([bjsm.bmj.com](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/11/695)). The six stage names here follow the AIS and ACC graded return |
| R6 Season review after a third concussion in twelve months | An athlete with `concussion_review_count` (default 3) or more concussions reported in any 365 days | AIS 2024 position statement: where more than two concussions occur within twelve months, consider missing the rest of the season of contact and collision sport ([ausport.gov.au](https://www.ausport.gov.au/concussion)). The check flags the review; the doctor and the athlete decide |
| R7 Safety checks for staff working with under-18s | An active staff member attached to a squad with an active athlete under 18, with no safety check expiry recorded or one in the past | New Zealand: the Children's Act 2014 requires safety checks of children's workers in state-funded services, and many sports require the same of every coach and staff member working with minors ([legislation.govt.nz](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2014/0040/latest/whole.html)). Australia: each state's working with children law, for example the Child Protection (Working with Children) Act 2012 (NSW) ([legislation.nsw.gov.au](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2012-051)). Record the check that applies to you in `safety_check` |
| R8 Complete injury records | An injury with no body area, mechanism or activity | The IOC consensus statement on recording and reporting epidemiological data on injury and illness in sport (2020) records location, mechanism and the activity at the time of onset for every case ([bjsm.bmj.com](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/54/7/372)). Without them the injury register cannot say where days are lost |

## What is not a rule

Training load and wellness flags are not compliance. The acute to chronic workload ratio, week-on-week changes and wellness drops are thresholds your performance staff set (`npm run athletes -- settings`), and they show on `/attention`, not here. The defaults (a ratio over 1.5, under 0.8, a 15% week-on-week jump, a wellness score four points under the athlete's own 28-day average, under six hours' sleep) are common starting points in the sports science literature, not standards.
